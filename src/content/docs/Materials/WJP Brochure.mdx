## WJP General Brochure
[Go to editable word document.](https://wjproject-my.sharepoint.com/:w:/g/personal/njardon_worldjusticeproject_org/IQAGg3LAOIV5QLRu0C4C6kT9AUR6uxivmXmeJ5Z9_HSE5RQ?e=7heSK2)

**Advancing the rule of law, globally.**
Communities with effective rule of law thrive with peace, justice, and equal opportunities. Corruption is reduced, poverty is mitigated, health promoted, the environment protected, and businesses sustained for growth and investment.
The **World Justice Project (WJP)** is an independent, multidisciplinary organization dedicated to advancing the rule of law across the globe. Headquartered in Washington, DC, with regional representations in Mexico City and Singapore, our work focuses on four critical streams: countering authoritarianism, advancing people-centered justice, driving private sector commitment to the rule of law, and strengthening environmental rule of law. 
We drive systemic change using a proven three-part methodology: Evidence, Co-Creation, and Local Action.

**Evidence**

*Clarifying where progress is made and attention is needed.*

Evidence is our foundation. WJP produces the world's most comprehensive rule of law datasets, relied upon by public and private sectors to drive justice globally. Our flagship WJP Rule of Law Index® is used by governments in more than 50 countries to guide development planning, integrated extensively by multilaterals like the World Bank, OECD, UNDP, and the European Commission, and utilized by Fortune 500 companies and risk agencies to inform global operations. Journalists from the The Economist, Financial Times, and The Guardian, and to the Estadao de S. Paulo, O Globo, and El Pais, rely on our data. Academics worldwide cite us in journals of economics, criminology, risk regulation, sustainability, social science and technology.
We understand justice systems broadly—looking beyong the courts to other actors that help people resolve legal problems. WJP data shows that 5 billion people worldwide suffer from unmet legal needs. To help institutions close this gap, we develop the analytical tools—including legal needs surveys in over 100 countries, the Legal Needs Atlas, and the People-Centered Justice Indicator Framework developed with the OECD and HiiL. 
At the country level, our subnational indexes and frameworks allow governments to understand, identify barriers, and monitor real progress.  Recent examples include the development of people-centered justice indicators in the Dominican Republic, outcome indicator frameworks in Thailand, long-term analysis of legal needs data in England and Wales, and a new initiatives to strengthen justice measurement and reform in Zambia. By generating better evidence, WJP helps institutions make better decisions.

**Co-creation & Dialogue**

*Building the trust and cooperation that reform requires.*

Data is our starting point for difficult conversations. We break down institutional silos, foster trust, expose systemic bottlenecks, and align stakeholders around unified strategies. We help create the political and social conditions that enable reform.
Globally, we partner with networks such as the OECD, OGP, the Justice Action Coalition and the SDG 16 Data Initiative. Our convenings unite the global rule of law community: the 7th World Justice Forum brought together over 600 attendees from 82 countries to counter authoritarian expansion, while our recent 2026 Business Forum on the Rule of Law in Tokyo convened leaders from 17 countries under the Chatham House Rule for candid, actionable dialogue. Nationally, we bring comparative evidence and practical recommendations directly to ministries, such as our ongoing collaboration with the Ministry of Justice of Uzbekistan to translate evidence into concrete reform strategies.

**Local Action & Technical Assistance**

*Translating local priorities into institutional reality.*

Evidence alone does not improve justice systems; institutions need practical support. WJP works with governments, judiciaries, ministries of justice, and local partners to improve institutional performance, strengthen accountability, modernize management practices, and expand effective justice pathways.
This includes developing open justice metrics, supporting institutional reforms, improving the use of alternative and community-based dispute resolution mechanisms, and helping institutions adopt technology that responds to real user needs. WJP’s Assessment Tool for ICT-Driven Reforms in Family Justice, developed with the World Bank, is one example of how we help institutions adopt technology strategically rather than simply digitizing existing processes. In criminal justice, WJP is also developing new measurement frameworks that help governments better understand state capacity, security challenges, and institutional performance. In Mexico, we helped create a legally binding public policy instrument to address due process and impunity and collaborated with local activists and the state prosecutor’s office to launch a live tracking platform. We are supporting Thailand’s path toward stronger institutions, greater public trust, and a rule of law system that works better for people. Together, WJP’s evidence and technical assistance strengthen the institutional pathway that leads to better justice outcomes.
