Justice systems affect people's ability to protect their rights, resolve disputes, access services, participate in economic life, and trust public institutions. Yet in many countries, justice remains difficult to access, fragmented, slow, and disconnected from the problems people face in everyday life.

**The World Justice Project (WJP) works to advance people-centered justice by helping justice systems deliver better outcomes for the people they serve.** We understand justice systems broadly—not only courts, but also legal aid providers, prosecutors, ministries, administrative agencies, customary and informal justice mechanisms, civil society organizations, and other actors that help people resolve legal problems.

# Our goal: Better justice outcomes
Over the next three years, WJP will contribute to three outcomes that define a people-centered justice system:
* Justice is more accessible, timely, and effective, enabling people to find help and access the mechanisms they need to resolve their legal problems fairly and efficiently.
* Justice institutions are more open and accountable, earning greater public trust through transparency, participation, and responsiveness.
* Justice is more people-centered, meaning that institutions design services and make decisions based on people's needs rather than institutional processes alone.

These outcomes are ambitious and require change across many parts of the justice ecosystem. WJP alone cannot achieve them. They depend on governments, justice institutions, civil society, development partners, and communities working together.

# How change happens
WJP's theory of change is based on three complementary pathways.

**1. Improving laws, policies, and incentives** 
Justice outcomes improve when legal frameworks and public policies create the right incentives for institutions to focus on people, measure results, and remain accountable.

**2. Strengthening institutions and justice pathways**
Justice systems become more effective when institutions have better data, stronger management, appropriate technology, and greater technical and financial capacity. Stronger institutions are also better able to work with the broader justice ecosystem—including legal aid providers, community justice mechanisms, and alternative dispute resolution systems—to deliver services that respond to people's needs.

**3. Shaping public understanding and behavior**
Justice reforms are more likely to succeed when policymakers, practitioners, donors, and the public share a common understanding of what people-centered justice means and why it matters. Stronger public narratives also encourage greater respect for the rule of law and sustained demand for better justice services.

# How WJP contributes
WJP contributes to these pathways through three mutually reinforcing areas of work.

**1. Building the evidence base for reform**
Evidence is the foundation for better institutions. Countries cannot improve what they do not measure.
WJP develops the data, indicators, and analytical tools that allow governments and justice institutions to understand people's legal needs, identify barriers, prioritize reforms, and monitor progress.
This work includes legal needs surveys conducted in more than 100 countries, the Legal Needs Atlas, and the People-Centered Justice Indicator Framework developed with the OECD and HiiL. Together, these initiatives are helping establish common standards for measuring justice outcomes and strengthening national justice data systems.

At the country level, this evidence supports practical reforms. Recent examples include the development of people-centered justice indicators in the Dominican Republic, outcome indicator frameworks in Thailand, long-term analysis of legal needs data in England and Wales, and a new initiatives to strengthen justice measurement and reform in Zambia.
By generating better evidence, WJP helps institutions make better decisions.

**2. Providing technical assistance to strengthen institutions**
Evidence alone does not improve justice systems. Institutions also need practical support to translate evidence into action.

WJP works with governments, judiciaries, ministries of justice, and local partners to improve institutional performance, strengthen accountability, modernize management practices, and expand effective justice pathways.

This includes developing open justice metrics, supporting institutional reforms, improving the use of alternative and community-based dispute resolution mechanisms, and helping institutions adopt technology that responds to real user needs. WJP's Assessment Tool for ICT-Driven Reforms in Family Justice, developed with the World Bank, is one example of how we help institutions adopt technology strategically rather than simply digitizing existing processes.
In criminal justice, WJP is also developing new measurement frameworks that help governments better understand state capacity, security challenges, and institutional performance.
Together, WJP's evidence and technical assistance strengthen the institutional pathway that leads to better justice outcomes.

**3. Supporting policy dialogue and public understanding**
WJP also contributes to the other two pathways by helping shape policies, standards, and public narratives.

At the global level, WJP works with organizations such as the OECD, the SDG 16 Data Initiative, the Justice Action Coalition, and the Ibero-American Alliance on Access to Justice to develop common standards, strengthen international commitments, and promote people-centered justice as a development priority.

At the national level, WJP supports policy dialogue by bringing comparative evidence, international experience, and practical recommendations to governments and justice institutions. Current collaborations, including with the Ministry of Justice of Uzbekistan, illustrate how WJP helps countries translate evidence into reform strategies.
Finally, WJP helps build public understanding of justice as a development issue. Access to justice affects poverty, health, education, housing, business development, and public trust. By communicating this broader vision, WJP helps create the political and social conditions that enable reform.

# What success will look like
Over the next three years, success will be measured by what WJP can directly deliver and influence: stronger evidence, practical tools, institutional support, and greater uptake of people-centered justice approaches.

WJP will aim to generate actionable evidence, help partners use data to set priorities and monitor progress, strengthen institutional capacity through technical assistance and training, advance common measurement standards, and communicate how justice affects people's lives and broader development outcomes.

Success will be reflected in whether WJP's evidence, tools, technical assistance, and policy engagement are used by partners to make justice reforms more informed, practical, and people-centered.
