## We are living in a rule of law recession.
For nearly a decade, WJP's data has tracked a consistent, global trend: the rule of law is weakening in the majority of countries. More than 60% of the nations we measure have seen their scores decline. The erosion is not uniform — it shows up differently in different contexts — but the direction is clear.

## What is behind this? 
The short answer: authoritarian trends. In many countries, the mechanisms designed to hold power accountable — independent courts, free press, auditing and oversight — are under pressure. Some are being dismantled openly. Others are being hollowed out more quietly, through appointments, budget cuts, or procedural changes that shift power without triggering obvious alarms.
Civic space is shrinking. Journalists, activists, lawyers, and civil society organizations face increasing restrictions on their ability to operate. In some cases, this means direct persecution. In others, it means a gradual narrowing of what's possible — more self-censorship, more risk, less room to challenge authority.
The dismantling the rule of law can be startingly quick, twice as fast as it takes to build a resilient rule of law system. 
The situation is serious — but it is not irreversible. The same data that documents the decline also shows that progress is possible. That the trend can be reversed. That the conditions for reform exist in more places than is commonly assumed. The urgency is real. So is the opportunity.
