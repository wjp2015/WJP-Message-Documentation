## Everyday People
<Card title="To a homemaker in the US / Economics" icon="seti:shell">
	The rule of law matters to your family because it shapes something as everyday as the price you pay at the pump. When conflict breaks out overseas prices can spike overnight, and that's often unavoidable. But what happens next depends heavily on the rule of law at home. It determines whether energy companies can use that chaos as cover to price-gouge, collude, or manipulate markets, or whether regulators and courts are actually able to step in and hold them accountable. It determines whether contracts between producers, refiners, and retailers get honored so supply keeps moving, instead of breaking down into hoarding and speculation that drive prices even higher.  
  **When the rule of law is strong, a global crisis still hurts — but your family isn't also paying a hidden markup because no one was watching, or because the watching didn't matter.**
</Card>

<Card title="To a homeless person in the United Kingdom / A2J" icon="telegram">
It matters when you don't have a fixed address or savings, and still need to challenge an unfair eviction, a wrongly denied benefit claim, or unlawful treatment by a landlord or official. It means legal aid is available and accessible, and that the police treat you with the same fairness as people who can afford a lawyer. 
**When the rule of law is strong, justice isn't a privilege reserved for those who can afford it — it's a system your family can actually use when everything else has broken down.**
</Card>

<Card title="To a taxi driver in Brazil / Safety & Corruption" icon="seti:lock">
The rule of law matters to you because it affects whether you can work and move through the city safely. It matters if criminal groups control certain areas, if the police abuse their power or fail to protect people like you, if corruption means you have to pay bribes, or if powerful people can break the rules while ordinary people pay the price. 
**When the rule of law is stronger, streets are safer, police are more accountable, rules are clearer, and people have real ways to seek justice when something goes wrong.**
</Card>

<Card title="To a construction worker in France / Labour" icon="seti:stylelint">
The rule of law matters to you because it helps ensure that your work, your safety, and your dignity are protected. It means your employer should follow safety standards, pay what you are owed, respect labor protections, and not take advantage of you because you need the job. It also means that if you are injured, mistreated, discriminated against, or denied your rights, there should be institutions you can turn to and rules that apply fairly — not only to workers, but also to companies, unions, regulators, and the government. 
**When the rule of law is strong, your livelihood does not depend only on the goodwill of someone more powerful.**
</Card>

### To the homemaker in the US - 2 min
**This is not about being a Democrat or a Republican.** It is about whether the basic rules that protect your family, your home, your community, and your future still work.
The rule of law is what keeps power from becoming personal. 
*It means that no president, governor, mayor, judge, police officer, company, or wealthy individual gets to make up the rules for themselves. 
*It means that courts should be fair, public officials should be accountable, the press should be able to ask hard questions, and ordinary people should have somewhere to turn when they are treated unfairly.

You may not think about the rule of law every day. Most people do not. You think about groceries, school, health care, gas prices, your mortgage or rent, your children’s safety, and whether your family can get ahead. But all of those things depend, in quiet ways, on rules people can trust. Families make plans when they believe the system is stable: that contracts will be honored, courts will be fair, businesses will not be punished for politics, and government agencies will follow the law.

When leaders attack judges, weaken watchdogs, intimidate the press, or say that some people deserve fewer rights, it may feel like just another political fight. But it is more than that. It is a warning sign that the rules may be becoming optional for people with power.
And once that happens, no one is fully protected.

Today the target may be someone you do not identify with — an immigrant, a protester, a journalist, a judge, a political opponent, or a minority group. But tomorrow it could be a school, a church, a business, a neighborhood, or a family like yours that needs the system to be fair.

The rule of law matters because it is what lets people disagree without destroying the country. It is what protects your rights when your side is out of power. It is what keeps government limited, communities stable, information honest, and the economy predictable.

So, this is not about politics. It is about common sense. No matter how you vote, you should want rules that apply to everyone, leaders who are accountable, courts that are independent, and a country where your family’s future does not depend on who has power today.


## Business
<Card title="To the micro-entrepreneur in Senegal / Regulation & Corruption" icon="rocket">
The rule of law matters to you because it affects whether your business can survive and grow. It matters when you sign a contract and need to trust that it will be respected. It matters when you apply for a permit and should not have to pay a bribe. It matters when a larger company, a local official, or someone with more influence tries to take advantage of you. 
**When the rule of law is strong, you can plan, invest, hire, and build your business with more confidence — because the rules are clearer, your rights are protected, and success depends less on connections and more on your work.**
</Card>

## Government
<Card title="To the public official in South Korea / Whistleblowing & Corruption" icon="comment">
The rule of law matters to you because it gives legitimacy, clarity, and stability to public service. It helps ensure that decisions are made according to clear rules, not personal pressure, political favoritism, or informal influence. It protects citizens from arbitrary decisions, but it also protects honest officials by giving them procedures to follow, standards to rely on, and institutions that can distinguish between lawful discretion and abuse of power. 
**When the rule of law is strong, government can act more effectively because people trust that public authority is being used fairly, predictably, and for the public good.**
</Card>
