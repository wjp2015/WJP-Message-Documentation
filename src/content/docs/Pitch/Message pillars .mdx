 <Aside type="note" title="Core ideas">
These 4 messages are the basis of all our communications.

<CardGrid stagger>
	<Card title="1. The rule of law is foundational to people’s everyday lives.">
        Most people do not think about the rule of law every day. They think about groceries, school, work, health care, safety, rent, transportation, their business, their family, and whether they can build a better future. 
        **All of those things depend, in quiet ways, on rules people can trust.**

        The rule of law is not an abstract idea. It is the foundation of dignity, security, prosperity. 
        **It is what makes freedom real. It is what makes justice possible. And it is what makes power accountable.**
    </Card>
	<Card title="2. Strengthening the rule of law is both achievable and urgently needed.">
		We are living in a rule of law recession.
        Data from the World Justice Project shows that over the past decade, the rule of law has weakened in most countries, driven by declining checks and balances, shrinking civic space, and growing demand for justice that systems are unable to meet.

        **The situation is serious — but it is not irreversible.** The same data that documents this decline also shows that progress is possible. There are examples. 
        
        * Every region has a country where rule of law is improving (Dominican Republic, Senegal, North Macedonia, Thailand, UAE, Bangladesh, Poland)
        
        * *If speaking to a country that has not improved, focus on sub-factors to show progress, and then revert to main challenges for urgent change.*

	</Card>
	<Card title="3. Progress requires a clear understanding of the challenges, strong cross-sector partnerships, and sustained political commitment">
		WJP generates actionable evidence that is then an objective entry point to build trust, convenes diverse stakeholders, and co-creates practical, locally grounded solutions. **Evidence, Co-creation, and Local action**. 
       
        Evidence acts as the anchor; hard facts allow us to sideline politicized and polarized rhetoric, it’s the starting point for difficult conversations to detonate change. 
        
        We break down silos among stakeholders, foster trust, explore underlying obstacles and bottlenecks together, and align around a unified, collaborative strategy. 
        
        We then focus on turning strategy into a binding policy that can ultimately improve people’s experience of the rule of law.
    </Card>
    <Card title="4. The rule of law matters">
		*Enumerate some examples, or refer to people narratives. Make sure to use at least three examples.* 
        
        The rule of law matters when:
            * a journalist investigates corruption without fear of retaliation.
            * a citizen knows that the judge is independent. 
            * people can trust that public information is not being manipulated to divide society or justify discrimination. 
            * a small business signs a contract and trusts that it will be enforced, even against a more powerful company. 
            * workers can use the law to protect themselves from exploitation or abuse. 
            * a family paying inflated gas prices because of corporate capture.
            * a woman is safe from violence, and she knows that the police will protect her.

        The rule of law sustains social systems in invisible, yet tangible ways. When it doesn't work, the costs show up everywhere — and they're rarely labeled as the rule of law, even if they are. **When the rule of law works, people speak freely, plan for the future, and invest in their businesses and communities.** 
    </Card>
